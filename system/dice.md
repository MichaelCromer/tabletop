# Dice Checks

This system resolves action outcomes through two kinds of rolls: **Tests** and **Attempts**. All dice are d6.

## The (Ability) Test

The most familiar to those who have played other TTRPGs. When your GM asks for an "(Ability) Test" to determine the outcome of an action, you roll two dice and add your character's relevant ability bonus to the total. If you meet a certain value representing the difficulty of the task, your action succeeds.

Your GM may have a specific difficulty value in mind, but the system defines the following defaults:
| Difficulty | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value | 7 | 9 | 11 |

Sometimes, environmental factors confer additional benefits or penalties to a Test. In these cases, your GM may ask for you to roll "at Advantage/Disadvantage". This means you roll three dice instead of two, removing the lowest and highest values respectively before calculating the total as normal.

This system uses Tests to simulate instantaneous actions with a chance for meaningful consequences, resolved in the moment; such as making a weapon attack, deciphering a mystic rune, or telling a lie. Outcomes are biased towards reliable middle values and benefit significantly from better ability scores.


## The Attempt

When your GM asks for an "Attempt", you roll (at least) one die and count the number of sixes rolled. The outcome depends on how many sixes you roll:

| Sixes | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 0 | Failure |
| 1 | Minor Success |
| 2+ | Major Success |

The GM tells you how many bonus dice you may add to your roll given the circumstances; more favourable conditions means more dice and a greater chance of success. Attempts can also be tied to one of your character's ability scores, in which case you add additional dice equal to your relevant ability bonus. Certain Skills may also grant bonus dice for certain Attempt actions.

Attempts come in two common subtypes, which determine how the outcome is interpreted.

### The Situation Attempt

A Situation Attempt is resolved at the end of the same game turn. A Minor Success yields the basic result; a Major Success yields a better result, or an additional bonus. Certain actions may define their own scales of success in place of this default.

This system uses Situation Attempts to simulate complex tasks with significant complicating factors outside of your character's control, typically ones that involve some kind of urgency sustained over a period of time; such as foraging for supplies, praying to the gods, or sailing through a storm. Outcomes are biased towards failure, unless circumstances are favourable -- or can be manipulated to be favourable.

### The Progress (Ability) Attempt

A Progress Attempt tracks your accumulated progress toward a future outcome. A Minor Success adds **1** to your progress tally; a Major Success adds **3**. You may repeat this Attempt on subsequent actions, tallying your progress as you go. With each consecutive attempt on the same task, the number of dice rolled increases by one. The task succeeds only after you meet a predetermined total, representing the difficulty of the task.

Your GM may have a specific difficulty total in mind, but the system defines the following defaults:
| Difficulty | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total | 3 | 6 | 10 |

If the effort is interrupted (you take a different action), the consecutive-attempt bonus is reset, but your accumulated progress is not. If there is a risk of meaningful failure, your GM may also rule that the attempt incurs complications, or even fails entirely, if you accrue too many failures before you meet the required difficulty total.

This system uses Progress Attempts to simulate prolonged tasks where further effort meaningfully increments progress towards some complex goal, particularly ones that can be set aside or re-attempted; such as crafting an item, negotiating a deal, or picking a lock. Outcomes are biased towards methodical, slow progress, but higher ability scores significantly shorten timelines.
