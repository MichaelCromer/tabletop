# Dice Checks

This system determines success and failure through three different kinds of dice rolls, called "checks". All dice used by checks in this system are a d6.

## The (Ability) Check

The most familiar to those who have played other TTRPGs. When your GM asks for an "(Ability) check" to determine the outcome of an action, you roll two dice and add your character's relevant ability bonus to the total. If you meet a certain value representing the diffculty of the task, your action succeeds.

Your GM may have a specific difficulty value in mind, but the system defines the following defaults:
| Difficulty | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value | 7 | 9 | 11 |

This system uses Ability checks to simulate instantaneous actions with a chance for meaningful consequences, and are resolved in the moment; such as making a weapon attack, deciphering a mystic rune, or telling a lie. Outcomes are biased towards reliable middle values and benefits significantly from better ability scores.


## The Circumstance Check

When your GM asks for a "Circumstance check", this means you roll one or more dice and count the number of sixes rolled. Any number of sixes is considered a success; in certain cases, more sixes yields a better result.  

The GM will tell you how many dice you may roll given the circumstances; more favourable conditions means more dice and a greater chance of success. Circumstance checks are not typically tied to one of your character's ability scores, unless at your GM's discretion, but your character may have special skills that grant additional dice for certain types of Circumstance checks.

This system uses Circumstance checks to simulate complex tasks with significant complicating factors outside of your character's control, typically ones that involve some kind of urgency sustained over a period of time; such as foraging for supplies, praying to the gods, or sailing through a storm. Outcomes are biased towards failure, unless circumstances are favourable -- or can be manipulated to be favourable.


## The Cumulative (Ability) Check

When your GM asks for a "Cumulative (Ability) check", this means you roll a number of dice equal to one more than your character's relevant ability bonus and count the number of sixes rolled. No sixes awards one failure, one six awards one success, more than one six awards three successes. You may repeat this check on subsequent actions, tallying the total number of successes as you go. With each consecutive attempt, the number of dice rolled increases by one. The action succeeds only after you meet a certain total number of successes, representing the difficulty of the task. If the effort is interrupted, the cumulative bonus dice are reset, but not your progress towards the goal. If there is a risk of meaningful failure, your GM may also have the attempt may incur complications, or even fail entirely, if you accrue too many failures before you meet the required difficulty total.

Your GM may have a specific difficulty total in mind, but the system defines the following defaults:
| Difficulty | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total | 3 | 6 | 10 |

This system uses Cumulative checks to simulate prolonged tasks where further effort meaningfully increments progress towards some complex goal, particularly ones that can be set aside and re-attempted later; such as crafting an item, negotiating a deal, or picking a lock. Outcomes are biased towards methodical, slow progress, but with higher ability scores significantly boosting timelines.
