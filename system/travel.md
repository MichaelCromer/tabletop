# Travel

Travel is one of the four modes of play with a formal ruleset.

The Travel rules govern time spent moving Overland; through neutral or hostile unpopulated regions where the players must fend for themselves. The Overland map is where players move between Locations of interest, scouting the path ahead, foraging for supplies, and avoiding dangers. It is possible to be moving Overland and not be in Travel (for example, a Pressure scenario crossing a rope bridge over a ravine), but the converse is not typical. However, if you wish, Travel can also cover movement through friendly territory between Settlements.

Travel costs the players resources in terms of supplies and equipment, and potentially health and other special conditions as they forge their way through the wilderness. Players may offset this by foraging for food, searching for shelter, or resting to recover, and these costs may increase if the players opt to take a more dangerous route.

The wilderness is not a passive participant in Travel mode. While the players are taking Travel actions, you as the GM will also be resolving environmental actions that directly influence the journey.

## Quick Guide

Here is a short summary of running Travel.

 - Announce the shift into Travel as soon as the players embark on a journey.
 - Narrate the situation: the time and weather; the terrain and visible features of the current tile, and of the neighbouring tiles, as needed.
 - Announce the start of a Travel turn and ask for player Travel actions. A Travel turn is ordinarily four hours. A player may make one action per Travel turn.
 - Resolve the Travel turn, narrating any changing circumstances. All actions in a single Travel turn are resolved simultaneously at your discretion; there is no specific order of events.
 - At the same time, make an Environment action. You may prefer not to announce or narrate these, depending on circumstances.
 - Continue to resolve Travel turns like this until Travel ends. This occurs as soon as the players arrive at a Location of interest (passing into Exploration mode) or safely return to a Settlement (passing into Downtime mode), or some other event briefly interrupts it (such as an ambush starting Pressure mode).

Some tips you might consider:

 - Players will often think of Travel in terms of numbers of tiles: days, not individual turns; legs between camps; and only a small number of foraging or scouting actions to support their movement. This will be more common in less threatening terrain where the players feel they can plan further ahead. This is natural, and you should allow your narration to stretch or compress as needed, while still tracking the four-hour Travel turn. Some Travel actions have been designed to span multiple turns for this reason.
 - Conversely, when the terrain becomes more challenging, expect the travel to become more granular, as the players begin to consider the cost of moving to a new tile more carefully and begin to delegate the increasingly important tasks of scouting and foraging.
 - If the environment does not imply scarcity, do not track a resource. For example, it may only be interesting to track water supplies while in Desert tiles, or shelter while in Mountain, Tundra, and other rough terrain. Otherwise, we can assume that water is plentiful enough that players can find sufficient supplies whenever they choose to Camp. However, you should consider carefully how much you want to do this -- pushing too much into the background may trivialise the survival aspect of wilderness travel.
 - Note the differences between the Scout and Search actions. The first is for when the players wish to learn general information about their surrounds: about food or shelter, about tracks or active hostile forces, about the best path ahead (for instance, whether there is a ford across a river). You may decide what, if any, of this kind of information that a Scouting player learns, but generally a Scout does *not* learn about well-hidden tile features, and Scouting does not generally benefit from repeated attempts. This is the purpose of the Search action, which is for locating a specific person, place, or thing, which the players must announce ahead of time, and which becomes cumulatively more successful the longer the Search is performed. It is up to you whether having first Scouted a tile confers any bonus to subsequent Search attempts.

## Travel Actions

Here is a list of Travel actions that players can take. You may wish to add or remove options to this list depending on the circumstances.

| Action | Description |
|---|---|
| Camp | Make camp in the current tile. |
| Conceal | Erase signs of your presence or movement. |
| Forage | Gather food, materials, or other supplies from the surrounds. |
| Move | Move to an adjacent tile. |
| Scout | Learn limited information about this or a neighbouring tile. |
| Search | Thoroughly investigate the current tile for features or secrets. |

### Camp

Camping is for when players wish to rest and recover, consuming supplies.

The players Camp (typically, players all Camp simultaneously) by announcing their intention to do so. If they could reasonably set up a campsite given the current circumstances, this automatically succeeds. If you as the GM decide that they are lacking in sufficient shelter or supplies, for example, you may require the players to succeed on a Scout, Forage, or even Investigate action before proceeding to Camp the following turn.

Camping consumes one unit of rations per player and, if circumstances demand it, one unit of camping supplies (firewood, etc). Camping takes one travel turn to complete. After expending resources like this, a Camping player may continue to Camp for the next turn at no additional resource cost.

The act of Camping covers multiple potential sub-actions, such as resting, studying, or standing guard; you may opt to narrate these sub-actions more freely than other Travel actions, as you might during Downtime. However, you shouldn't take the narrative pressure off completely, since the Overland is significantly more hazardous than a Settlement.

Each player must complete two Camp actions per day in order to avoid fatigue. Each successful Camp action replenishes a player's Action Points by one. Additionally, players may make a Cumulative (Might) roll at the end of each consecutive Camp action to recover from Wounds and other long-term status effects.


### Conceal

Concealment is for when players wish to avoid detection.

A player Conceals by describing how they hide their presence: covering tracks, stashing gear, muffling sound, or otherwise obscuring signs of their activity. They then make a Circumstance check; better cover or dim lighting grants bonus dice, while open ground or a larger group imposes penalties.

Each six rolled determines the quality of concealment:
- **1 six:** Signs of your presence are reduced. Enemies must succeed on a Circumstance check to track the party through this tile.
- **2+ sixes:** Your trail is well-hidden. Only a deliberate Search of the tile will reveal evidence of the party's passage.

A Conceal action can be used prior to Camping if the party wishes to set up a hidden campsite, or prior to Moving if they wish to travel unsees. A successful Conceal check can only provide cover for one subsequent action.


### Forage

Foraging is for when players wish to gather supplies from their surrounds.

A player Forages by declaring what type of supplies they are foraging for; for example, "food", or "firewood". They then make a Circumstance roll. The base difficulty covers Foraging in the most challenging of circumstances, so clement weather or less wild surrounds should confer some bonus. As GM, you should also consider allowing Foraging to synergise with Scouting, so that players Foraging in a tile that has been Scouted can benefit from the party's better knowledge of the terrain.

Some suggested base dice counts are given in the following table.

| Terrain | Dice |
|---|---|
| Plains   | 3 |
| Desert   | 2 |
| Forest   | 2 |
| Hills    | 2 |
| Tundra   | 2 |
| Jungle   | 1 |
| Mountain | 1 | 
| Swamp    | 1 |
| Water    | - |

Each instance of Foraging takes one Travel turn to complete. The number of units Foraged of the stated resource is equal to the number of sixes rolled.

### Move

Movement is for when players wish to travel to a neighbouring tile.

The players Move (typically, players all Move simultaneously) by indicating which neighbouring tile they wish to travel to. If no environmental factors bar their way, this automatically succeeds. If you as the GM decide that something would reasonably block or delay their journey (such as needing to find a bridge to cross a wide ravine), you may require the players to succeed on a Scout or Search action before proceeding to Move the following turn.

Different terrain types have different difficulties associated to their Move action. The base turn costs of Moving *into* a new tile are given in the following table.

| Terrain | Cost (turns) |
|---|---|
| Plains   | 1 |
| Desert   | 2 |
| Forest   | 2 |
| Hills    | 2 |
| Tundra   | 2 |
| Jungle   | 3 |
| Mountain | 3 | 
| Swamp    | 3 |
| Water    | - |

 - **Water** tiles are impassable without a vessel or some other means of traversing them.
 - **Roads** connect tile centres together. Following a road reduces the travel time to enter a tile by 1 turn (minimum 1).
 - **Rivers** follow tile edges. Crossing a river without a bridge or ford increases travel time to enter a tile by 1 turn.

### Scout

Scouting is for when players wish to learn general information about this or a neighbouring tile.

A player Scouts by announcing which tile they would like to investigate. They then make a Circumstance roll. The base difficulty covers Scouting in the most challenging of circumstances, so clement weather or less wild surrounds should offer some bonus dice. Scouting a neighbouring tile is more difficult than Scouting the current one, and the Scouting player does not Move to that tile. Each instance of Scouting takes one Travel turn.

Some suggested base dice counts are given in the following table.

| Terrain | Dice |
|---|---|
| Plains   | 3 |
| Desert   | 2 |
| Forest   | 2 |
| Hills    | 2 |
| Tundra   | 2 |
| Jungle   | 1 |
| Mountain | 1 | 
| Swamp    | 1 |
| Water    | - |

Success is measured by the number of sixes rolled. One six reveals basic information (terrain, obvious features, signs of significant presences). Two or more sixes reveals more detail (specific resources, hidden paths, precise locations of hazards). At your discretion, you may ether determine randomly what each subsequent Scout action uncovers, or tie it to the player's stated intent. You may also decide whether repeated attempts on the same tile may reveal new information.

The following is an example of what sort of information a successful Scout action can reveal about a tile:
 - the locations of food and water sources, or shelter;
 - the presence and nature of environmental hazards;
 - tracks or other signs of passage and habitation;
 - the location and nature of river crossings, mountain passes, or other favourable paths; or,
 - evidence of hostile forces.


### Search

Searching is for when players wish to locate a specific feature within the current tile.

A player Searches by announcing exactly what they would like to find in the current tile. They then make a Cumulative (Wit) roll against the difficulty of locating this feature. Each instance of the Search action requires two Travel turns, and can only count as a success if these turns are taken consecutively and are uninterrupted. Players searching simultaneously may pool their successes together.

## Environment Actions

Each Travel turn, choose or roll for environmental developments. Each time the players successfully Move to a new tile or successfully spend resources Camping, roll a Cumulative check. On a success, something changes in the environment that affects the players' journey. As GM, it is up to you to determine exatly what this means for your game.

| Action | Description |
|---|---|
| Weather | The weather changes. |
| Hazard | Something in the natural environment poses a challenge. |
| Encounter | The players encounter a (potentially hostile) force. |

### Weather

Generate weather appropriate to the region and season. At minimum, note whether conditions are **clear**, **cloudy**, **rainy**, or **stormy**. You can use a d6 roll against a terrain-specific table if you prefer random generation.

Harsh weather imposes a penalty die on Circumstance checks and may increase Move or other Travel action costs (stormy conditions add +1 turn to all Move actions).

### Hazards

 rockfall, flash flood, quicksand, a collapsed bridge, or similar. Resolve hazards as an Ability check or Circumstance check as appropriate. A hazard may block passage until dealt with, cost resources to bypass, or inflict a status effect on failure.

### Status Effects

Status effects accumulate from environmental exposure, missed Camps, or hazard failures. Each has three tiers of severity.

**Hunger** — accrues when a player goes a full day without a Camp action that includes a meal.
- **Hungry:** Cannot regain Action Points from Camping.
- **Famished:** Disadvantage on all Might checks.
- **Starving:** Take 1 Wound per Travel turn until fed.

**Fatigue** — accrues when a player gets fewer than two Camp actions per day.
- **Tired:** Cannot regain Action Points from Camping.
- **Fatigued:** Disadvantage on all Circumstance checks.
- **Exhausted:** Cannot take Move actions.

**Exposure** — accrues in extreme temperatures without appropriate shelter.
- **Cold / Hot:** Cannot regain Action Points from Camping without shelter.
- **Freezing / Burned:** Disadvantage on all checks.
- **Frostbitten / Sunstruck:** Take 1 Wound per Travel turn until treated.

A single Camp action in appropriate conditions reduces one status by one tier.
